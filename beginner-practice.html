Beginner Golf Practice: Your First Four Weeks

New to golf? This beginner golf practice plan is the same one Coach Priya Lindqvist uses in our first-timer clinics. It takes about 45 minutes, three times a week, and it is built so that you leave every session hitting the ball a little better than when you arrived.

What to bring to your first practice session

Three clubs, not fourteen

A 7-iron, a pitching wedge and a putter are all you need for the first month. Fewer clubs means fewer decisions and more repetitions with each one.

Comfortable shoes and a glove

Sneakers with a flat sole are fine to start. A glove on your lead hand helps prevent blisters while your grip pressure settles down.

A few tees and a towel

Tees make it easier to get the ball airborne in week one, and a towel doubles as a target for chipping practice.

A note on your phone

Write down the date and one score from each session. It takes thirty seconds and shows you how far you have come.

Range etiquette every beginner should know

Driving ranges are friendly places, and a few simple habits help you fit right in.

Stay behind the line

Only hit from your own mat or between the markers, and never walk out to pick up a ball, even one that rolled only a few feet.

Give neighbors room

Leave an empty bay between you and the next golfer when you can, and check behind you before every practice swing.

Take your time

Nobody is watching as closely as you think. Pausing between shots to reset is good practice, not slow play.

A sample 45-minute beginner practice session

PartTimeWhat to do
Loosen up5 minutesArm circles, gentle trunk turns, and ten slow practice swings with no ball.
Short swings10 minutesHip-high to hip-high swings with the pitching wedge. The only goal is to brush the mat and get the ball in the air.
7-iron to a target15 minutesPick a flag and make three-quarter swings. Count how many of every ten shots get airborne and head roughly toward it.
Chipping8 minutesFrom just off the practice green, chip with the wedge onto a towel a few steps away.
Putting7 minutesShort putts from three feet, then a few long ones focused only on rolling the ball close.

The four-week plan

Week 1: Contact

Use the sample session above, teeing up every ball. Success is simply hitting the ball solidly more often than not.

Goal: 5 of 10 wedge shots airborne.

Week 2: Off the tee

Hit half your 7-iron shots off a tee and half from the mat. Most beginners are surprised how quickly the gap closes.

Goal: 5 of 10 7-iron shots airborne from the mat.

Week 3: Aim

Lay a club on the ground pointing at your target to check your alignment, then hit every shot at a specific flag.

Goal: 4 of 10 shots finish closer to your flag than to the next one.

Week 4: Around the green

Spend half the session chipping and putting. This is where new golfers save the most strokes in their first rounds.

Goal: 6 of 10 chips stop on the green.

Coach Priya's tip: Stop the session while you are still hitting it well. Ending on a good shot makes you excited to come back, and coming back is what turns beginners into golfers.
