Golf Improvement Drills That Carry Over to the Course

These are the golf improvement drills our coaches assign most often. Each one targets a single skill, takes about ten minutes, and comes with a pass mark so you know when you have earned the right to move on.

Full swing drills

Feet-together drill

Stand with your feet touching and hit half-swing shots with a 7-iron. If you sway or lunge, you will lose your balance right away. This drill teaches you to turn around your spine instead of sliding.

Pass mark: 8 of 10 shots finish in balance and get airborne.

Pump drill

Swing to the top, pump halfway down and back up twice, then hit the ball on the third move. It trains the lower body to start the downswing while the arms wait.

Pass mark: 7 of 10 shots start within a stick's width of your target line.

Towel under the arms

Tuck a towel under both armpits and make three-quarter swings. If the towel falls out, your arms are working separately from your body.

Pass mark: towel stays in place for 10 straight swings.

Nine-shot challenge

Try to hit a low, middle and high shot, each with a draw, a straight flight and a fade. You will not pull off all nine at first, but trying them teaches you how the clubface controls the ball.

Pass mark: 5 of the 9 shapes on your first attempt.

Chipping and pitching drills

Landing spot towel

Lay a towel on the green about one-third of the way to the hole. Your only goal is to land the ball on the towel and let it roll the rest of the way. This shifts your focus from the hole to where the ball should land.

Pass mark: 6 of 10 chips land on the towel.

Ladder drill

Place tees at 10, 20, 30 and 40 yards. Hit one pitch to each tee, then work back down. Changing distance on every shot builds feel instead of muscle memory for one swing.

Pass mark: every ball finishes within 3 steps of its tee.

Lead-hand-only chips

Chip with only your lead hand on the club. It keeps the hands ahead of the clubhead and stops the wrists from flipping through impact.

Pass mark: 7 of 10 chips make clean contact.

Up-and-down game

Drop five balls around the green in different lies. Chip each one, then putt it out. Count how many you finish in two strokes. This is the closest thing to real scoring pressure you can create in practice.

Pass mark: 2 of 5 for mid handicappers, 3 of 5 for low handicappers.

Putting drills

Gate drill

Push two tees into the green just wider than a ball, about a foot in front of it. Roll putts through the gate without touching the tees. It proves your face is square at impact.

Pass mark: 15 in a row through the gate.

Clock drill

Place six balls in a circle three feet from the hole, like the numbers on a clock. Make all six in a row. Miss one and start over. This builds confidence on the putts that matter most.

Pass mark: all six made, twice in a row.

3-6-9 lag drill

Putt from 30, 60 and 90 feet. You are not trying to hole them, only to stop each ball within a three-foot circle around the cup. Long-distance control is what removes three-putts.

Pass mark: 7 of 9 putts finish inside the circle.

Fringe stop drill

Putt toward the edge of the green and try to stop the ball as close to the fringe as possible without touching it. Without a hole to aim at, you focus entirely on speed.

Pass mark: 5 of 10 stop within a foot of the fringe.

How to build a practice session around these drills

Coach Devin uses what he calls the "two-thirds rule": spend the first two-thirds of a session learning, and the last third testing.

PartTimeWhat to do
Warm-up10 minutesEasy wedge swings, stretches, and a few half-speed swings with the weighted trainer if you have one.
Learning block25 minutesPick one drill from this page and repeat it with the same club and target until you hit the pass mark.
Testing block15 minutesPlay an imaginary hole on every shot. Change clubs, change targets, and go through your full routine each time.
Notes5 minutesWrite down your drill scores and one thing you want to work on next time.
Track it: Keep a simple notebook or phone note with the date, the drill, and your score. After a month, you will be able to see exactly which drills are making you better and which ones you have outgrown.
